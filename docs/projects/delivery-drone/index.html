The story below follows the practical work: understand the build, coach the operator in Arabic, prepare the aircraft and then progress to mission and payload-release setup.

Start with the person as well as the aircraft

The client had bought a heavy-lift drone, but it was not flying. He was new to drones and unfamiliar with the flight modes and operating workflow. My role was to work through the aircraft with him while teaching the process from the beginning, in Arabic, so the instructions were useful to the person carrying out the work.

Make remote work practical

Everything was done through calls and screen sharing. I reviewed the build, prepared the parameters and guided him through calibration and setup on the computer connected to the aircraft. The system used a CUAV X7+ flight controller and a SIYI transmission system specified for up to 30 km; that product rating is separate from the range demonstrated by this mission.

Build confidence before adding a mission

I made the first steps easier by using Loiter mode for more stable position-holding practice. We worked through what each mode did and how to operate the aircraft before progressing to a planned mission. The difficulty was not simply changing parameters: the client needed a sequence he could understand, repeat and use in the field.

Prepare the power system and the release

Support included the batteries and charger, not only the flight controller. I explained the charging workflow for the mission setup, which used a reported 12S 40,000 mAh LiPo configuration. We also configured the servo-operated gripper and its mission action for the 5 kg bag of rice. The component close-ups below show different stages and battery packs from the project.

Plan the delivery, then review what happened

Once he was comfortable with the aircraft, I taught him how to prepare the mission. The client completed the payload drop and the drone returned successfully. The matching log review records 20.25 km of total out-and-return travel and a 10.11 km maximum displacement. This is roughly a 10 km outbound mission, not a 20 km one-way delivery.

Use the difficulties to improve the next test

The work involved explaining unfamiliar modes, checking the build remotely, guiding calibration and connecting the release mechanism to the mission. The log review also records a radio-failsafe transition to RTL, vibration and accelerometer clipping, and current readings that need calibration before they can support energy estimates. I keep those findings in the engineering record alongside the successful delivery outcome.

What the project taught me

A useful remote handover has to work for the person on site. In this case, teaching in Arabic, reviewing the screen together and progressing from setup to Loiter practice and then mission preparation turned the support into a practical operating workflow. The photographs and recordings preserve those stages; matching mission evidence is shown separately in the report.
