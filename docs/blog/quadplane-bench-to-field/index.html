Building a 2.2 m QuadPlane as one connected system

Lift propulsion, cruise propulsion and avionics had to work together before field testing.

Achraf RAHIBE · 28 October 2026 · Project retrospective

The challenge: two flight regimes, one aircraft

The Cube Orange QuadPlane combined four lift motors with separate forward propulsion. That made the integration broader than mounting a flight controller. Airframe assembly, power distribution, control electronics and radio links all had to fit into one testable aircraft.

Working through the integration sequence

I integrated the structural components, propulsion, autopilot electronics, GNSS, RC and telemetry, then worked through sensor calibration and configuration. ArduPilot and PX4 workflows formed part of the development work. Bench checks established a baseline before control tuning, transition work and mission testing in the field.

The result and the next evidence

The delivered work included an assembled aircraft and an iterative sequence of configuration and field tests. Existing photographs show the fuselage integration, bench setup and aircraft in the field. I keep those stages visible because they explain the engineering behind the aircraft without turning a photograph into a performance claim.

Project record

My published portfolio describes field-test and mission-validation work. Photographs show the aircraft and integration stages; they do not independently quantify transition performance or payload capacity.
