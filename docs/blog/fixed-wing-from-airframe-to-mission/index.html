Taking a fixed-wing aircraft from airframe to mission

Different airframes require different integration decisions, even when they share an autopilot.

Achraf RAHIBE · 28 October 2026 · Project retrospective

The challenge: configuration is a system decision

My fixed-wing work spans V-tail, pusher and twin-motor layouts. The work begins with the aircraft as a whole: its structure, propulsion arrangement, control surfaces and the interfaces needed for the intended mission. Reusing a parameter file is only a starting point when the mechanical layout and propulsion change.

Connecting design and flight controls

I worked on airframe, propulsion and avionics integration, including differential-thrust configurations. Gazebo and software-in-the-loop gave me a way to examine mission and autopilot behaviour before moving into physical flight testing. I also connected mission planning with camera and operator-view requirements, including DJI O4 Pro integration.

What the work delivered

The outcome was integrated aircraft configurations, simulation workflows, autonomous mission setup and field-test support. These were different development configurations rather than one aircraft with every feature. The project page brings their engineering scope and existing photographs together; more build-specific images can be added as the collection grows.
