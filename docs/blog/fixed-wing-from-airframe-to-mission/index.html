Printing and integrating a twin-pusher fixed-wing aircraft

Tornado V2 airframe fabrication, Pixhawk 6C integration and the checks behind field testing.

Achraf RAHIBE · 28 October 2026 · Project retrospective

Start with the airframe and its interfaces

For these builds I used the Titan Dynamics Tornado V2 airframe design. My work covered printing and assembly, propulsion and avionics integration, configuration and testing. The two pusher motors and conventional tail distinguish this aircraft from the four-lift-motor QuadPlanes elsewhere in the portfolio.

Balance printing weight with build practicality

I printed the shown airframe builds in ABS using 3% infill and one perimeter. Those were my lightweight fabrication settings for this build, rather than a universal strength recommendation. The photographs follow the work from wing and fuselage sections on the printer to laid-out parts and assembled aircraft.

Connect propulsion, surfaces and avionics

I integrated the Pixhawk 6C, twin propulsion paths, navigation and onboard wiring. The supplied technical material identifies ArduPlane 4.6.3 and a twin-pusher control-surface checklist. The integration work included surface directions, stabilization response and differential-thrust behaviour, with configuration and log review linked to the next checks.

Keep the public evidence useful

The open-fuselage views show the hardware integration, while the assembled aircraft images show the finished build stages. The log-review visual has its satellite map and identifying information removed. It provides illustrative review context, not exact flight-path or performance data.

Project record

The photographs document my printing, assembly and avionics integration of Titan Dynamics Tornado V2 airframes. The 3% infill and single-wall ABS settings describe my build configuration; structural strength is not quantified here.
